# Lesson 2: Anatomy of a Website & The Design Process

Welcome to Lesson 2! In Lesson 1, we covered how the road system (the internet) and the mail service (the web) work. Today, we're diving into the "buildings" you visit once you arrive: **websites** and **web design**.

---

## 1. What is a Website? (The "Digital Building")

Think of a **website** as a digital building. Your goal as a designer is to make the building sturdy, functional, and great-looking so people enjoy visiting.

* **A Web Page:** A single room in the building. It has a specific purpose (like a kitchen or a bedroom).
* **The Website:** The entire house. It’s all the rooms (pages) joined together under one address (like `yourbrand.com`).
* **The Homepage:** The front door and foyer. It’s the first thing people see, and it guides them to where everything else is located.

---

## 2. The Toolkit: HTML, CSS, & JavaScript (The "Building Materials")

You use three main tools to build every digital room:

| Tool | The Analogy: Building a House | The Definition | What it Controls |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **HTML** *(Hypertext Markup Language)* | **The Framework/Skeleton**<br>The wooden beams, concrete floor, and walls. | The structural framework of a webpage. | Where text goes, where images appear, where headings and paragraphs start and end. |
| **CSS** *(Cascading Style Sheets)* | **The Interior Design**<br>The paint color, wallpaper, furniture style, and lighting. | The visual styling of a webpage. | Colors, fonts, layouts, column structures, spacing, and background styling. |
| **JavaScript** | **The Utilities/Interactivity**<br>The light switches, running water, and automatic doors. | The programming language for dynamic behavior. | Interactive maps, pop-up windows, image sliders, form validation, and animations. |

---

## 3. What is Web Designing? (The "Architecture Plan")

Web designing isn't just writing code — it's about solving problems and creating a great experience for users. Web design splits into two primary roles:

### A. User Experience (UX) Design (The "Architect")
UX design focuses on **functionality** and **how it feels to use the site**. 
* **The Key Question:** *"Is this building easy to navigate? Are the hallways logical?"*
* **The Job:** Mapping out the user's journey, creating a sitemap (a blueprint of all pages), and making sure the site is accessible to everyone.

### B. User Interface (UI) Design (The "Interior Designer")
UI design focuses on the **visual look** and **atmosphere**.
* **The Key Question:** *"Does this building look modern and welcoming? Are the colors and typography right?"*
* **The Job:** Choosing color palettes, selecting typography (fonts), designing buttons, and creating visual mockups of each page.

---

## 4. The Design Process: From Sketch to Screen

Professional web designers don't just open code editors and start typing. They follow a step-by-step process:

1. **Define:** What is the goal of the website? Who is the target audience (e.g., teens, seniors, gamers)?
2. **Research:** Look at existing sites for inspiration. Identify what works well and what doesn't.
3. **Wireframe:** Draw a skeletal, black-and-white sketch of where things go. Focus on layout and structure, not color.
4. **Mockup (UI Design):** Apply colors, fonts, icons, and real images to the wireframe to create a pixel-perfect preview of the site.
5. **Code (Web Development):** Turn that final design into a living, clickable webpage using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.
